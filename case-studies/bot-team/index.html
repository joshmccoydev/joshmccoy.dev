I run my own work with a team of 15 AI assistants

The problem

AI is easy to start with and hard to trust. One chat helps with an email, another with a spreadsheet, and pretty soon nobody knows what got done, what got sent, or what it cost. I was doing everything myself, and the bot and AI work was unorganized and hard to keep track of.

What it does

I run 15 AI assistants, and each one has one job. There's a chief of staff, a sales lead, an editor, a designer, a researcher, an engineering lead, a builder, a CFO, an inbox manager, a calendar coordinator, a librarian and a social media manager, plus a few for my own life, like health and a bot that designs new bots.

On Oct 26, 2026 I retired 8 older bots and renamed the rest, so every role has one clear owner.

How it works

  1. One shared notebook. Everything the bots know lives in one Obsidian vault: a status page where each bot keeps its own section, a goal sheet per bot, a work board, a lessons log and a change log.
  2. Written rules. The rule is "ask first, always." Nothing goes out as me unless I ask for that exact message. No buying anything, no moving money, no passwords. If a source is missing or stale, the bot stops and tells me instead of guessing.
  3. Drafts, then my yes. My sales bot drafts outreach emails, my editor bot cleans them up so they sound like me, and nothing gets sent until I approve it.
  4. Cheapest tool that works. Simple checks run as free scripts. Cheaper models do the bulk drafting and sorting. The main assistant only handles judgment calls and talking to me.
  5. One place for questions. When a bot needs a decision from me, it goes on one reminders list on my phone, so nothing gets buried in a chat.
  6. A paper trail. Every routine logs one line per run: when it ran, how it went and what changed.

I don't have an exact number yet. By my rough estimate, the team saves me about 5 hours a week on the routine work alone: the daily picks runs, sorting my mail, and watching for replies.

What it shows

None of this takes a computer science degree. It's clear jobs, written rules and a habit of checking the work. Those are the same things that make a good crew run, and any owner can learn to set them up for their own business.
